The premise

Static test beds make evaluation look cleaner than the underlying construct.

Benchmarks often compress a broad capability into one fixed sample, one perturbation regime, and one reported score. This work asks what changes when evaluation itself becomes a source of evidence: inputs vary, conditions are explicit, runs are repeated, and results are reported as distributions rather than isolated points.

Research arc

Three papers. One shift in how we measure.

The progression is deliberate: first introduce stochasticity, then expose dimensions of robustness, then generalise the evaluation logic beyond embedding benchmarks.

PTEB generates meaning-preserving paraphrases at evaluation time and aggregates performance across multiple stochastic runs. The result is an evaluation that measures how stable an embedding model remains under natural variation rather than treating one fixed test set as definitive.

Measurement-as-Distribution

Evidence should scale with the claim.

The MD protocol makes five stages explicit. The aim is not to replace every benchmark with a distribution, but to make the assumptions and evidence behind an evaluation inspectable.

C

Construct

Specify what capability is being measured and what claim the evaluation is intended to support.

G

Generate

Create the evaluation evidence with a principled, reproducible data-generation process.

Φ

Perturb

Introduce validated variation that preserves the intended construct while testing sensitivity.

E

Evaluate

Repeat evaluation across generated conditions so uncertainty and variability become observable.

R

Report

Report distributions and stability alongside point estimates, matched to the breadth of the claim.

P =( C, G, Φ, E, R )construct · generation · perturbation · evaluation · reporting

What the programme reveals

Three recurring observations.

01

Rank and score can tell different stories.

A model may retain its relative ordering while its absolute score shifts under valid perturbation. Reporting only one view can conceal that distinction.

02

Robustness has multiple axes.

HTEB shows that lexical / stylistic, length and language variation can produce partly decoupled robustness profiles rather than one universal notion of “robustness”.

03

More evaluation evidence changes the question.

Once inputs and evaluation conditions vary, the object of interest becomes a distribution of measurements — and the strength of a claim can be matched to the evidence supporting it.

From papers to practice

A practical evaluation workflow.

  1. Define the construct. State what the benchmark is intended to measure.
  2. Choose meaningful variation. Identify transformations that test the construct without changing it.
  3. Validate the transformations. Check quality and preservation rather than assuming generated inputs are valid.
  4. Repeat the evaluation. Treat stochasticity as evidence, not noise to discard.
  5. Report the distribution. Show score variation, ranking stability and the conditions that produced them.
